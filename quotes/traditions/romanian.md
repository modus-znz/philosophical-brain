## Heritage

Romanian wisdom sits at the crossroads of the Balkans, the Carpathians, Latin
Europe, and the Orthodox East. Its folk soul is the *folclor* of doinas,
proverbs, and fables; its philosophical voice — Cioran, Ionesco, and the
sculptor-architect Brancuși — is playful, paradoxical, and built for hard
earth. The proverb "vorba dulce" (the sweet word) runs through it: wit is
defense, endurance is pride.

## Signature Sayings

> "Vorba dulce mult aduce." (The sweet word brings much.) — Romanian proverb

> "Cine sapă groapa altuia, cade el însuși în ea." (He who digs another's grave falls into it himself.) — Romanian proverb

> "Omul sfințește locul." (The man sanctifies the place.) — Romanian proverb

> "Ce-i în mână nu-i minciună." (What's in hand is no lie.) — Romanian proverb

> "De la obște pe ogor, dar fiecare cu brazda lui." (From the common field, but each with his own furrow.) — Romanian proverb

> "Cine se scoală de dimineață, departe ajunge." (He who rises early travels far.) — Romanian proverb

> "Viața trebuie trăită înainte de a fi înțeleasă." (Life must be lived before it can be understood.) — Romanian rendering of [[Søren Kierkegaard]], *Journals* IV A 164 (1843)

## Code Directive

Say the sweet word and keep the good faith — Romanian craftsmen know that
deception mints only short-term coin. Dig no one's pit in a code review, and
take the furrow you own. Rise early, work the common field fairly, and let
your hands be your proof.
